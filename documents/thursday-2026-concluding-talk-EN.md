## Opening: gratitude and the week ahead

Congratulations for maintaining such an excellent standard in all the construction projects.
Clearly it was the event of the year — the event of the century: we had the international
convention in Bucharest, and very many of you supported the work there. I saw you working hard;
some of you, months in advance, had many video conferences and connected for the preparations —
right in the thick of it all. You worked hard despite the heat, and surely everyone missed part
of the program. But it's good that now we can catch up. Let's get on with the stream, and be
assured that Jehovah greatly cherishes the efforts and sacrifices you have made.

On top of that, in the preaching ministry you keep showing a special kind of love: [...] 28
regular pioneers, and an army of auxiliary pioneers and publishers keep working tirelessly. It's
clear you love Jehovah, and many sincere people in the territory are hearing the truth — some of
them thanks to you. And when harder times come — as with sister Ștefanida, when she was affected
by a flood and the firefighters came — you were ready to jump in and help. Whenever someone in
the congregation faces difficulties, you want to be a loving family. Surely there would be many
more reasons to commend you.

A newly baptized one, Răzvan Brădiceanu, has been baptized since the last visit — I even remember
sharing in his study with Bogdan last time. Now he's our brother, and beautiful studies keep being
conducted. Congratulations! We can't wait to work alongside you this week and to get to know both
you and the friends of the truth better. And let me not forget the up-and-coming Romani
congregation, which is making excellent progress. After you benefited from a substitute overseer
who spoke Romani even better than I do, the group is revived, energized — and I understand that
fine progress is being recorded.

Speaking of the Romani work: the Sibiu Mediaș Est congregation, which I visited last week, has a
Romani group that, starting September 1st, will function as a Romani congregation. They've united
with Târnăveni — two groups became one, Târnăveni together with Mediaș — so from September 1st,
in a short while, they will function as a congregation. We're waiting for the day when in Sibiu
too we'll have a Romani congregation.

**This week's program:** every day from 10 a.m. we go out into the ministry — every morning,
Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday, at 10:00, here at the Kingdom Hall. And obviously, if the
engineers help us and get Zoom running, we'll turn on Zoom too, for those who have health problems
or for other reasons can't make it. We have 22 connections — I greet you too, Petrică; thank you
for connecting. We'll also try to broadcast the field-service meeting points, and even the meeting
with the pioneers, for those doing auxiliary or regular pioneering who can't come in person.

Since I've mentioned the meeting with the pioneers — it's good for you. There were two options:
Thursday afternoon at 5:30, or Saturday around 12:30, for lunch. You know there are programs here
every evening, but if we come at 5:30 it takes about an hour, and by the time the brothers arrive
for 7 p.m. we're already leaving — so, preferably, Thursday evening at 5:30, or we leave it for
the weekend, Saturday around noon. *[Show of hands:]* How many for Thursday evening at 5:30,
option one? How many for Saturday at 12:30? That settles it.

So on Saturday we go out into the ministry, and then we'll see — maybe we do it right at 12:00.
If it's very hot, a real heat wave, maybe we pull back around 12 and hold the meeting here. On a
day that hot, at exactly 12:00 — don't come in earlier — Saturday at 12:00 sharp, the meeting with
all the pioneers. Don't forget: auxiliary, missionary, regular — everyone, everyone — Saturday at
12:00.

For Sunday, here's what we have at 10:00: the public Bible talk, **"What to Do to Live Forever."**
What can you do to live forever — that's the theme of the day. Invite all the interested ones,
and remind them they can also connect on Zoom — but at 10:00. And also on Sunday, after the
Watchtower [study] — don't become slaves to your own problems.

## Theme: the good news

Tonight we're talking about the good news. What are you willing to do for the good news? What news
do people hear? I'd say daily — morning, noon, and evening, on TV, on the radio, on the internet —
news, news, news: news about wars, about fires, about drought, about the energy crisis — and only
negative. Who is there to give people something good, some good news? Fortunately, on a global
scale, there is an energetic, dynamic people — volunteers, Kingdom proclaimers — who, without
being paid, are willing to give people something good. And we are talking about the good news of
the Kingdom.

We're not talking about just any piece of news. True, it's the best in the world — there is none
better — but we're talking about the truth: about what has changed our own lives, about what can
bring a ray of light and hope into people's lives. And that depends on our attitude toward the
good news. Please, let's open together to 1 Corinthians 9:23. When you've found it, we'll read.

## Making a comment — the art of teaching

Making a comment at the meeting means the ability to make a synthesis, a summary. You heard a
theme in the material — try to jot down that theme in a single sentence, or two or three, not the
whole thing. When you respond, you may have a whole paragraph in front of you; but when you comment
in your own words, you pass that information through the filter of reason.

Now, why aren't we like some teachers? We don't reproduce the entire content. Some of us know we
were even taught wrongly, made to cram, because back then you had to learn everything by heart.
But when we go into the preaching ministry, we want to teach people from the Bible — and you see
that people don't have time for you to tell them everything you know from the Bible. You have to
summarize it in just a few words. As we saw in the demonstrations our sisters prepared —
congratulations, very, very beautiful, thank God — it's important to know how to express yourself
briefly, to state the truth beautifully: the fundamental principles, but in few words, expressed
simply.

For that you need this skill — not only to read correctly, but also to write correctly, in the
sense of taking notes, because that's how you learn. You learn to express your ideas after passing
them through the filter of your mind, and even of your feelings, so that when we go into the
ministry we don't transfer information from our heads to people's heads, but from heart to heart.

A second idea, then: learn to read and write, and after that, develop communication skills. How do
you like to communicate — through messages, or on Facebook? That's artificial. Stand in front of
the person, converse with them, speak with them. For those of us who are older it's clear — we
like to communicate, to socialize, to tell stories. But I challenge the new generation to
communicate face to face, because they really do prefer texting. I've even seen young people in
love — a boy and a girl sitting on a curb or a bench, each with a phone in hand. I don't know
whether they were texting each other declarations of love — as if they weren't there for each
other, but for the phone. The preaching work can't be done that way. True, on Zoom we do write
messages too. But it's very important, as we saw, to relate to people — because when we
communicate, we don't just talk. What does communication mean? To listen. To see things through
the eyes of the person, to hear with their ears, to feel with their heart. As the young woman from
Germany said, "I was concerned only with myself and my feelings, and I had no joy in the ministry."
So develop communication skills; show empathy and compassion — toward the puppy, toward the cat,
toward the person, toward everything around you.

## The goal: start a Bible study

As for you — regarding communication, the art of teaching, the manner of speaking with people —
let's set ourselves the objective, for instance, of conducting at least one Bible study. We were
told, on the occasion of the visit of the representative from world headquarters: we go into the
ministry, true, but we no longer take notes, we forget, we no longer know where there was
interest, where there was a refusal. So let's resolve to remember at least one person, to go into
the ministry with this objective. This week let's keep this goal: we go out, and if we manage to
talk with a person, let's remember them and go back to keep encouraging them. The objective of
starting a study is a very good one.

And for the sisters, too, exceptional prospects have opened up now — sisters can more easily
approach the women. In the past it was said you needed strong men, because they worked in the
printery; but now sisters are needed as well — in construction, as missionaries, and for other
assignments. So sisters can enjoy these privileges for a long time. Don't call it settling,
though — because at some point one may want to fall in love, to marry, to have children. That's
perfectly fine. If you're single and haven't yet found a partner, put these things first, work on
the goals we've talked about; and when you do give it thought — perhaps after Bethel, after the
school, after a few years of pioneering — a word of caution: to start a friendship with a boy,
observe him from a distance before he knows you're interested in him, and ask a few questions.
Does this boy love Jehovah? What goals does he have? Does he have responsibilities in the
congregation, and how does he fulfill them? How does he treat his mother, his grandmother? How
does he generally speak about women, about the older ones, about the younger ones? What reputation
does he have in the congregation? Once you have the answers to these questions, you can begin a
period of getting to know each other.

## To grandparents and parents: raise your children to work

Dear grandparents, dear parents — first of all, we're addressing you. Do you have children? Put
them to work. This is an essential requirement — they must also learn to read and write correctly,
and not only the girls; the boys too must learn to work.

Why is this so important? Because it's from them that missionaries, special pioneers, and Bethel
workers will come. If you raise your children so the boys have a trade — they can do something with
their hands — then even if a crisis comes and they lose a job, they won't go hungry. It matters
enormously to know a trade. And afterward, in the meantime, they'll have completed their
studies — they'll get a master's, they'll be PhD candidates, no problem — but they'll be happy
people if they also know how to work with their hands and develop well physically too.

If we look around us, it's harder and harder to find [good tradespeople]. I have nothing against
it — you can find an engineer, but it's ever harder to find a good plumber, a good electrician, a
good mechanic. Cars break down; they need maintenance; and it doesn't take many years to learn a
trade like that. That's why, with the children — and, as we saw, the boy should also know how to
cook, wash the dishes, and clean — it's very important, because then he has self-respect: a man of
worth. And besides, he knows how money is earned. A little financial education, as you saw, doesn't
hurt: let the boy put something into the envelope himself — at least money for food, for bills, for
electricity, for gasoline. After he reaches a certain age, not everyone should go asking their
parents and grandparents for money; let him earn his own, because then the boy has self-respect —
he's more decided, more energetic, more joyful, happier when he can manage on his own. That's why
it's important for young people to be responsible, and, as we saw, to develop discernment and the
capacity to think, so they don't fall into the trap of materialism — because then they'd miss out
on a theocratic course of life.

And it's very important to cultivate humility. For instance — true, as we saw in the last video —
the young man said he waited until he was 18. That's right; it's a legal matter. The Bible doesn't
say a boy must necessarily be 18 to become a ministerial servant, but the law is the law, and so
after 18 a man can receive an appointment as a ministerial servant, and then as a congregation
elder, already around the age of 20. But the young man in the video was disappointed when he turned
18 and didn't receive the appointment. So it's very good — I encourage young people to want to
aspire to privileges: to start pioneering, to become ministerial servants, to become missionaries,
to want to be circuit overseers, for example. The organization now wants us to have younger circuit
overseers — not old ones like me; we're a generation of overseers who are a bit past our expiry
date — [overseers] under 30. Yes, we need capable, intelligent overseers who are very good with
technology, who are healthy, energetic, vigorous — but above all, brothers who love their brothers.

So it's good to want privileges, but you must ask yourself the question: *why* do I want to become
a missionary, an overseer? In Jehovah's organization — the organization is not a plant, not a
factory, not a company — [you want it] because you love Jehovah and you want the brothers among
Jehovah's people to be well cared for. So when you have privileges, it's that you want to love
Jehovah more, to serve the brothers, to make them feel good and be protected. You young men who
have privileges — want to lead Jehovah's people into the new world, to protect them, so that not
one of them is lost along the way, neither the inactive one, nor the one removed from the Kingdom
Hall. Let us be ready to search for them and gather them in. As we saw, there were the verses in
Jeremiah too: such shepherds is what Jehovah wants in these days — ones who love the brothers, who
would give their lives for them, who would do anything to see them happy, so as to bring them into
the new world. If that's the motivation, it works — and it's very important.

So take on responsibilities, and don't forget Luke 16:10, a principle: "The person faithful in
what is least is faithful also in what is much." You know the principle. So the elders will entrust
you with various responsibilities. Maybe you think, "Yes — they've put me on cleaning, to scrub the
toilet," or "they'll put me on the microphones," or on repairs and maintenance. And here, by the
way — congratulations, your congregation excels at supporting the construction projects; whenever
something is needed, you get involved. But whoever does the small things well, whoever is faithful
in little things, will also receive greater responsibilities — because there it shows that any
activity connected with the theocracy is worthwhile. So whether you're sisters or brothers — we're
all brothers — let's get involved and do everything within our power, with all our heart, for
Jehovah's work; let's do everything for the good news. Let's follow the third part of the material.

## Closing encouragement: to the older ones

Dear brothers and sisters on Zoom who are of a certain age — and also you who are here in the hall
and are of an age, no longer 20 or 30 like the young people in the video — Jehovah loves you
immensely. You did everything for Jehovah when you dedicated yourselves and got baptized, some of
you half a century ago, and you have persevered. You did everything within your power back then.
And now, don't say, "But now I can do less" — now, do everything you can. As much as you can do,
do; and through your perseverance you have contributed to the spread of the good news. Without you,
the truth would not have reached us. Your zeal is exceptional — it's like an engine for the new
generation.

Now the young ones are rising. And now it's our turn to do everything that depends on us. Never
forget: there weren't nearly as many opportunities in the past as there are now for young people.
We'd have wanted them too, back then, but they didn't exist. Now it's better. Take Bethel: in the
past you went to Bethel and stayed there your whole life; not now. Now there are reviews at 30, at
40, at 50. Some go at 18 and leave at 30; others stay until 40; some go for only six months, others
for a year or two. They receive training, they become rooted in the faith, they come to look to
Jehovah more, to understand better how the organization works — and then they leave Bethel and make
room for others. A rotation is made, so that far more young people can pass through Bethel than
otherwise could. After that comes the school, then the missionary work, then the traveling work;
some will be selected for that. And there's construction — as we saw, the LDC. There are so many
possibilities, so don't hesitate to put yourself in the path of the spirit and do the same.

Step by step, as our older brothers did, do everything that depends on you. Maybe you think, as
some have said, "I don't feel very capable"; maybe our steps are small; maybe we're shy or unsure.
But the important thing is to be heading in the right direction. The important thing is to
understand that we are part of something far greater than we ever imagined. You are part of
Jehovah's people; you are a friend of the Sovereign of the Universe; you take an interest in
Jehovah's purpose; you are very precious. What matters is to allow Jehovah to pour out countless
blessings upon you.
